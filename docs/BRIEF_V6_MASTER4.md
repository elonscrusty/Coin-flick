## 02. Locked game design and interaction ownership

The public lobby is shared. Players can enter a solo hunt or form a private party of up to four participants. Each hunt contains exactly one shared central source pile of individually identifiable whole rocks. Players collect rocks, visibly carry them, deposit them at their assigned processing station, and open them there. Exactly one rock is the hidden geode target for that round. The first station to complete valid processing of that target wins. Picking up or depositing the target does not end the round.

Every participant has their own processing station around the common pile. These stations are equipment workspaces, not private rock sources. Ordinary rubble is credited to the participant responsible for processing it. The winning player receives one persistent specimen. Other participants keep their own ordinary earnings but do not receive consolation geodes. Solo discoveries increment the existing solo-find measure; they must not inflate multiplayer wins. The shared lobby gallery displays actual owned specimens rather than generating rewards from decoration.

Manual tools operate over a rock on the station plate. The progression remains Starter Hammer, Steel Hammer, Jackhammer, Auto Cracker, Hydraulic Press, and Industrial Crusher, according to actual existing entitlement and balance configuration. Automatic equipment consumes its owner's deposited queue while that owner collects more rocks. It does not harvest directly from the mound, steal another player's records, or process every rock in the arena. One active processing mechanism controls one current rock at a station, preventing manual and automatic duplicate resolution.

The server decides rock ownership, valid actions, processing completion, reward identity and round resolution. The client provides intent and presentation. Target information stays server-only before resolution. Neutral whole-rock exteriors cannot disclose the winning rock through glow, special color, different label, predictable placement, visible identifier, or an exception to a full-storage rule. Cosmetic specimen appearance can be revealed after the winning completion.

Retain configurable starting capacity values where appropriate. The earlier design used a five-rock carry basket and ten-rock station queue. The latest recording displays five carry slots and a ten-rock table queue, which aligns with that design. It also displays a rubble capacity of one hundred, apparently reflecting the current upgraded configuration. Do not reset that to thirty because an older example used thirty. Carry capacity, station queue capacity and rubble bag capacity are distinct concepts and must remain separately displayed and validated.
